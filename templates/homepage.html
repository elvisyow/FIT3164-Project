How Our Elo Works

The Elo system used on this site is a custom elo rating to measure the skill level of Tennis players. Every player starts with a base rating of 500, representing an average skill level. After each match, both players’ ratings are updated based on who won and who was expected to win.

The expected win probability between Player A and Player B is computed using the Elo formula: 

    E_A = 1 / (1 + 10^((R_B - R_A) / 400))

- R_A and R_B are the current ratings of Player A and Player B.
- E_A is Player A’s expected probability of winning the match.
- The number 400 is the scaling factor that controls rating sensitivity.

Once the match result is known, the ratings are updated as follows: 

    R'_A = R_A + K × (S_A − E_A)
    R'_B = R_B + K × (S_B − E_B)

- K is the K-factor, which controls how much ratings change per match (our model uses K = 32).
- S_A is the actual result for Player A (1 for a win, 0 for a loss).
- E_A is the expected score from the first formula above.
- Both players’ ratings always change in opposite directions, keeping the system zero-sum.

This ensures that:

  • Beating a higher-rated opponent gives a larger rating boost.
  • Losing to a much lower-rated opponent causes a bigger drop.
  • Over time, players’ Elo values stabilize to reflect their real performance level.

The same logic powers our match predictions and historical trend charts. The system can be extended with surface-specific adjustments, tournament weighting, and decay over time to better capture player form.
